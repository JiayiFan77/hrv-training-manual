What is HRV?

Heart Rate Variability (HRV) refers to the beat-to-beat fluctuation in the time interval between consecutive heartbeats (the RR interval). Higher HRV generally reflects greater autonomic nervous system flexibility and better physiological readiness to train.

Why HRV matters for athletes
  • Assess daily training readiness and recovery status
  • Monitor cumulative fatigue from training load
  • Support individualised periodisation decisions
  • Early detection of overtraining syndrome or illness

Core Metrics

MetricFull NameWhat it reflects
RMSSDRoot Mean Square of Successive DifferencesParasympathetic (vagal) activity — most commonly used in sport science
SDNNStandard Deviation of RR intervalsOverall autonomic regulation; influenced by both branches of the ANS
LF/HF RatioLow-Frequency / High-Frequency Power RatioSympatho-vagal balance; elevated ratio suggests sympathetic dominance
pNN50Proportion of RR differences > 50 msSimilar to RMSSD; reflects parasympathetic activity
HF PowerHigh-Frequency Power (0.15–0.4 Hz)Respiratory sinus arrhythmia; strongly linked to vagal tone

Standard Measurement Protocol

HRV data quality is highly dependent on consistent collection conditions. Adhere strictly to the morning protocol below. Small deviations compound into misleading trends over time.

URA Morning Measurement Protocol
  1. Wake up and lie supine for 5 minutes — no conversation, no phone
  2. Put on the chest strap (Polar H10 or Garmin HRM-Pro preferred); moisten electrodes slightly
  3. Open your HRV app and begin a standard 3–5 minute recording
  4. Breathe naturally — do not attempt to control breathing rate or depth
  5. Measure at the same time, in the same position, every day
  6. Log subjective wellbeing: sleep quality (1–5), fatigue (1–5), muscle soreness

Effect of Body Position on HRV

PositionExpected HRVRecommended use
Supine (lying)Highest — strongest vagal toneGold standard; best repeatability
SeatedSlightly reducedAcceptable for mobile / field use
StandingNoticeably lowerUsed specifically for orthostatic testing

Kubios HRV — Step-by-Step

Step 1 · Import Data

1
Open Kubios HRV → File > Open → select your data file.
2
Supported formats: .txt (RR interval list in ms), .csv, and device exports (Polar, Garmin, Suunto).
3
Confirm signal length and sampling in the left-hand panel before proceeding.

Step 2 · Quality Check & Artefact Correction

1
Inspect the RR interval time-series plot for outliers (ectopic beats, movement artefacts).
2
Enable Artifact Correction → Moderate (recommended default). Use None only for pristine lab ECG data.
3
Ensure the usable data segment is ≥ 3 minutes (5 min preferred for frequency domain analysis).

Data Interpretation

HRV varies enormously between individuals. Absolute values cannot be meaningfully compared across athletes. Always interpret relative to each athlete's personal baseline.

Interpretation Reference (relative to personal mean)
  • Normal range: within ±1 SD of rolling mean — continue planned training
  • Mild suppression: 1–2 SD below mean — reduce intensity, prioritise recovery
  • Significant suppression: >2 SD below mean — consider rest day; rule out illness or overtraining
  • Unusually elevated HRV: not always positive — extreme elevation may indicate early illness or parasympathetic rebound

Trend Analysis Principles

✓
Use a 7-day rolling average to smooth daily noise before drawing conclusions.
✓
Contextualise with training load metrics (TSS, sRPE, ACWR).
✓
A sustained downward trend over multiple days carries more clinical weight than a single low reading.

📄 Reference: Plews et al. (2013) — Training Adaptation and HRV, IJSPP

Troubleshooting

ProblemLikely Cause & Solution
Noisy / unstable signalPoor electrode contact → moisten strap before wearing; check strap tension
Abnormally low HRVActivity before measurement; insufficient rest; re-check protocol timing and posture
File format not recognisedFormat mismatch → confirm device export format; convert to plain .txt RR interval list
High batch-to-batch variabilityInconsistent conditions → audit measurement time, posture, strap model across sessions
Frequency domain metrics missingKubios Standard lacks full frequency features → upgrade to Premium or switch to Python
